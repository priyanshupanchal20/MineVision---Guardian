1. System interconnection

Sensors on the ESP32-S3 never share a UART. Thermal and the visible camera live on the Pi. Both boards share GND and a 5 V 3 A rail. Do not power the Pi from the ESP32 5V pin.

5 V 3.3 V GND UART / data Wi-Fi / USB

2. Power distribution

Never feed the Raspberry Pi from the ESP32 5V pin. Share GND. Use a 5 V 3 A supply. Star-ground all sensor blacks to the PSU negative.

Optional: AMS1117-3.3 from the 5 V rail + 100 µF if the radar browns out during Wi-Fi TX. 100 nF ceramic on every module VCC.

6. HC-SR04 left and right

Both modules are 5 V. TRIG from 3.3 V GPIO is accepted. ECHO is 5 V — never connect ECHO straight to the ESP32.

7. Mandatory ECHO voltage divider (make two)

ECHO — 2.2 kΩ — GPIO — 3.3 kΩ — GND. High level ≈ 5 × 3.3 / (2.2+3.3) = 3.0 V. Safe for ESP32-S3.

8. Waveshare S3KM1110 / HMMD 24 GHz (UART0, 115200)

Most Waveshare HMMD modules are 3.0–3.6 V only. 5 V can destroy the radio.

9. NEO-6M GPS (UART2, 9600)

UART crossing below matches firmware: Serial2.begin(9600, SERIAL_8N1, 15, 16) → ESP RX=GPIO15, ESP TX=GPIO16. Do not use the pads labelled TX and RX — those are already the TFMini-S. Indoor gps_valid=false is normal. Not centimetre RTK.

The u-blox NEO-6M chip is 3.3 V. Only put 5 V on VCC if the breakout has an onboard regulator (silkscreen often says “3.3–5V”). A 3.3 V-only board + 5 V will kill it. TX/RX are 3.3 V logic — do not assume GPS TX is 5 V tolerant into the ESP32 (clone TX is usually 3.3 V, which is correct).

10. BME280 (I2C0) — YD-ESP32-S3 pins 8 and 9

I2C is not crossed: SDA→SDA, SCL→SCL. Do not use silk TX/RX (those are TFMini-S). Firmware: Wire.begin(8, 9), addresses 0x76 and 0x77.

Power from board 3V3, not 5Vin, unless your BME280 breakout has a regulator and the silkscreen says 5 V is allowed. The sensor chip is 3.3 V.

10b. MPU-6050 (I2C1) — pads 17 and 18, not 8/9

BME280 and the 1.3" OLED share pads 8 and 9. MPU-6050 uses a second I2C bus on pads 17 (SDA) and 18 (SCL). Those were freed when TFMini-S moved to silk TX/RX. Firmware: Wire1.begin(17, 18).

VCC = board 3V3, not 5Vin. Tie AD0 to GND (address 0x68). Do not unplug the BME280 or OLED from 8/9 to free MPU pins — MPU has its own bus.

11. LM393 LDR module

Mount sky-facing, not in the LED glare. If raw values invert on your board, swap the map() ends in firmware.

12. Operator LEDs and buzzer

12b. 1.30" IIC OLED cab alerts (SH1106 128×64)

This is the module you have: front silk VCC GND SCK SDA (left → right). SCK is I2C clock (SCL), not SPI. Four wires. Shares I2C0 with the BME280. Runs on the ESP32 even if the Pi is unplugged. Do not use pads 17/18 (MPU-6050).

Test sketch: firmware/tests/10_oled_i2c/. Peel the blue protective film before judging contrast.

13. Raspberry Pi 3 Model B — 40-pin header (used pins)

Enable I2C: sudo raspi-config → Interface Options → I2C. Confirm with sudo i2cdetect -y 1 (expect 33).

14. MLX9064X thermal (MLX90640 / MLX90641) — Raspberry Pi 3, not ESP32

Do not put this on ESP32 pads 8/9 (BME280 + OLED) or 17/18 (MPU-6050). The 32×24 (or 16×12) array is processed on the Pi. Same 4 wires for MLX90640 and MLX90641. I2C address 0x33.

VIN = Pi 3.3 V (physical pin 1) only. 5 V will destroy the array. I2C is SDA→SDA, SCL→SCL — not crossed. Enable I2C: sudo raspi-config → Interface → I2C.

16. Assembly order and common ground

  1. Build both ECHO dividers on protoboard and verify ~3.0 V with a 5 V pulse (or continuity) before touching the ESP32.
  2. Connect all GNDs to the PSU negative (star), including Pi GND if you share the 5 V buck.
  3. Wire 3.3 V sensors (BME, LDR, radar) and power the ESP32 from USB. Run Phase 2 tests one device at a time.
  4. Add 5 V sensors (LiDAR, ultrasonics) on the 5 V rail, not from a weak USB-UART port if current sags.
  5. Connect Pi + MLX90640 + USB cam on their own 5 V input. Join GND to the ESP32/PSU star.
  6. Wi-Fi: phone hotspot or Pi AP. Set GATEWAY_HOST to the Pi IP.

UART discipline

Always TX → RX and RX → TX. I2C is SDA → SDA, SCL → SCL.

Cable length

LiDAR and radar < 50 cm. I2C < 30 cm. GPS antenna clear of metal and the ESP32 antenna.

Firmware match

These GPIO numbers are exactly firmware/include/config.h. If you move a wire, change that file and the matching test sketch.
